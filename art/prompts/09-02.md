# 09.02 · پناه دادن سیمرغ، زال را

## Arc

From the exposed rock into the Simurgh’s shelter, then through years of care to the white-haired youth. Protection is enacted by the bird, never by a depicted deity.

All scenes belong to chapter `08 · منوچهر`. Source text: `text/shahnameh.json`.
Each central light leads into the next painting; no frame, text or religious figure.

SUBJECT: The Simurgh leaves the high nest, her flight curved gently down toward the small wrapped infant Zal on an exposed rock. The bird notices the child; her two chicks are only dim small shapes in the distant nest. A single central glint beside the infant suggests harsh sunlight translated into a night miniature’s gold, no separate bright sun. The baby is safe and discreetly wrapped, crying with a small open mouth; no explicit nudity. Zal is infant age.
The Simurgh is one great manuscript bird with an elegant curved neck, a small ivory hooked beak, one calm dark eye, turquoise and malachite wing feathers edged in dull shell gold, and a flowing lapis tail with small vermilion tips. A restrained gold crest curves backward. Her two natural bird wings and two feet are distinct, never extra limbs; her claws cradle gently when carrying Zal. She is larger than a person yet remains a small outlined figure in the landscape, never a close-up, never a phoenix made of flame, never a human face, never a halo. Her own gold edging stays dimmer than the central light.
Zal, son of Sam, a human of unusually fair skin, dark almond eyes with white eyelashes, vermilion lips and a straight nose. His hair is pure ivory-white from birth, never blond, silver-grey or dark.
