# 04.01 · تهمورس → پادشاهی تهمورس: illustration prompts

Forty-six couplets. Tahmuras, Hushang's son, takes the throne and means to
wash the world of evil. He teaches wool and weaving, tames the cheetah and
the caracal and the hawks, brings the hen and the rooster gently to people,
and listens to Shahrasp, who fasts and prays and turns him from evil until
the farr shines. He binds Ahriman by a spell and rides that darkness around
the world. The divs rise; he breaks them, binds two thirds, and puts the rest
down with the mace. They buy their lives by teaching writing. He reigns
thirty years beyond that and dies. The toil remains.

This follows 03.01, which ended with Hushang still at the foot of an empty
seat. The threshold is the same seat, now with an indigo cloth beside the
leopard skin: the son's reign has not begun, but the cloth is waiting.

Paste Tahmuras's prompt line unchanged wherever he appears. Ahriman is never
a body: only the smudge in his sheet. The dead black div of 02.01 does not
return. The div host uses that body-type at crowd scale and faces the
viewer's right. Writing is taught with blank geometric pages: no letters in
any script. No depiction of God, the Prophet or the Imams.

Each centre is the doorway into the next painting. The brightest point is
small, white-gold, and at the exact centre. People stay miniature. Key
content stays inside the central 45% of the width. Full-bleed, no frame.

## Style paragraph

Persian miniature painting, Timurid–Safavid manuscript style (Herat/Tabriz
school, 15th–16th century), opaque mineral pigments and shell gold on
burnished paper, fine brushwork, flat stacked perspective, no Western
shading. Night palette: deep lapis-lazuli and ultramarine, glowing gold leaf,
small vermilion accents, malachite and turquoise, ivory. Visible paper grain
and slight gold craquelure.

## Closing lines

Strictly no text, letters, calligraphy, numerals, border, frame, margin or
signature. Not a 3D render, not photographic, not fantasy concept art. No
depiction of God, the Prophet or the Imams. No face, no hand and no writing
in the sky. Landscape 3:2, 1536 by 1024. The single brightest point is a
small white-gold light at the exact centre of the frame.

## Characters

**Tahmuras, prompt line (unchanged):** Tahmuras, Hushang's son, the div-binder, at miniature scale. The house face made a man's: dark almond eyes, a long straight nose, a firmer jaw than Hushang, a small closed mouth. A short dark beard, squared at the chin, not the three long white locks of Kayumars. Dark hair to the shoulders, held by a thin gold fillet. A narrow gold diadem, a simple band with one small plain gold disc, worn over the fillet; never a tall crown, never a turban. An indigo robe with a thin turquoise hem, a saffron sash, ivory trousers, and a narrow leopard-skin shoulder piece, pale tawny with black ringed rosettes, only on the left shoulder, not a full mantle. A heavy plain gold mace with a smooth ox-head and no engraving, at his side except when he lifts it. A soft pale-gold disc of farr behind the head, always dimmer than the painting's central light. He always faces the viewer's left.

**Ahriman, prompt line (unchanged):** Never a body. Only a tall smudge of lamp-black and ultramarine behind the black div, with no face, no eyes and no hands.

**Ahriman, 04.01 note:** The saddle and the rider sit on that same smudge. No horns, no wings, no muzzle.

**Div host (type, not the dead div of 02.01):** Wingless. Charcoal-black skin with a dull lapis sheen, not glossy. A wolf's muzzle, two white tusks, two short horns curving back. Eyes are two small vermilion dots, not lamps. Three dark claws on each hand. No clothes, no armor. In a crowd, only a little taller than a man, flat manuscript scale. They face the viewer's right.

**Shahrasp (this section only; not a king):** A slender older man. Narrow face, short iron-grey beard, no diadem, no leopard skin, no farr. An ivory robe with an indigo hem, a plain brown felt cap, bare feet. He stands. He never sits on the throne.

### 00-gah

No people.

SUBJECT: The seat after Hushang, before his son sits. The same low stone seat
on the night mountain. The leopard skin, pale tawny with black ringed
rosettes, is still folded there. Beside it, not yet worn, lies a folded
indigo cloth with a turquoise hem. On the cloth rests a narrow gold diadem,
a simple band with one small plain gold disc, and that disc is the only
bright thing. A heavy plain gold mace with a smooth ox-head leans in shadow,
dimmer than the disc, with no engraving. One cypress. Spongy rocks run off
every edge. No people, no div, no body.
COMPOSITION: The diadem's disc is a small white-gold point at the exact
centre. Seat, cloth and skin stay inside the central 45% width. Full-bleed,
no border.

### 02-pashm

Characters: Tahmuras, prompt line. One tiny spinner, no competing face.

SUBJECT: Wool becomes cloth. A fleece, ivory and pale gold, lies over a rock.

no letters, lies folded. Tahmuras stands beside the fleece, the same robe and
one-disc diadem, mace at his side. A sheep, flat and small, stands dim at the
edge. Night. No loom with inscribed parts.
COMPOSITION: The white-gold point on the thread is at the exact centre and is

### 03-yuz

Characters: Tahmuras, prompt line.

COMPOSITION: The gold ring of the lead is at the exact centre and is the

### 08-gorz

Characters: Tahmuras, prompt line. The div host.

SUBJECT: A short manuscript battle, not a horror image. Tahmuras, small, in
the same indigo robe and one-disc diadem, brings the ox-head mace down. Where
it meets a div's guard there is a small white-gold spark and nothing gorier:
no viscera, no severed head. Several divs are already seated and bound with
plain cords, alive, heads lowered. Others are only knocked down. Tiny scale.
Night mountains. Men, if any, are two leopard-less courtiers in indigo, dim,
at the back. The divs face right; Tahmuras faces left.
COMPOSITION: The spark at the mace is at the exact centre and is the brightest
point. Tahmuras and the struck div stay inside the central 45% width.
Vermilion is only the divs' small eye-dots. Full-bleed, no border.

### 09-dabir

Characters: Tahmuras, prompt line. Two divs of the host type, seated, bound
no longer, smaller than him.

SUBJECT: The divs teach writing, and the painting shows no writing. A gold
lamp. On a cloth, sheets of ivory paper carry only a geometric star and a
circle, never a letter, never a rune, never a seal with script. Three small
bowls of pigment: lapis, vermilion, gold. A reed pen lies unused across the
paper, with no mark yet drawn as a character. Tahmuras leans in, the same
face and robe, one hand near the page. Two wolf-muzzled divs sit opposite,
one claw pointing at the geometric star, not at a word. The mood is a lesson,
the heart of it the lamp.
COMPOSITION: The lamp's flame is at the exact centre and is the brightest
point. Faces, page and lamp stay inside the central 45% width. No glyph
anywhere. Full-bleed, no border.

### 10-yadgar

Characters: Tahmuras, prompt line. He is still, not wounded.

SUBJECT: The end of thirty years. Tahmuras lies quiet at the foot of the stone
seat, eyes closed, the same beard, indigo robe and one-disc diadem, no wound.
The seat holds the folded indigo cloth. Dim, small, and off to the sides so
they do not steal the light: a spindle, a hawk's perch, the ox-head mace.
A low gold lamp on the ground is the only brightness. A bare tree and a tree
in leaf, side by side, unmarked. Empty lapis sky, no face. The work remains;
the man is done.
COMPOSITION: The lamp's flame is a small white-gold point at the exact centre.
The still king and the seat stay inside the central 45% width. Full-bleed, no
border.
