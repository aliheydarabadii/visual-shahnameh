Seventy-four couplets. Jamshid, Tahmuras's son, takes the gold crown. He is
king and mobed, and for a long age the world obeys. He softens iron into arms,
teaches cloth, and sets the four estates: priests on the mountain, warriors,
free farmers, craftsmen. Divs mix clay and raise walls, baths and palaces. He
finds gems, perfumes and medicine, and crosses the seas in ships. He builds a
jewelled throne the divs lift into the air, and that day is Nowruz. For three
hundred years no one dies. Then he looks and sees no one but himself. He tells
the elders that bread and sleep and kingship are his. The mobeds are silent.
The farr departs. His day goes dark.

## Style paragraph

Persian miniature painting, Timurid–Safavid manuscript style (Herat/Tabriz
school, 15th–16th century), opaque mineral pigments and shell gold on
burnished paper, fine brushwork, flat stacked perspective, no Western
shading. Night palette: deep lapis-lazuli and ultramarine, glowing gold leaf,
small vermilion accents, malachite and turquoise, ivory. Visible paper grain
and slight gold craquelure.

## Closing lines

Strictly no text, letters, calligraphy, numerals, border, frame, margin or
signature. Not a 3D render, not photographic, not fantasy concept art. No
depiction of God, the Prophet or the Imams. No face, no hand and no writing
in the sky. Landscape 3:2, 1536 by 1024. The single brightest point is a
small white-gold light at the exact centre of the frame.

## Characters

**Jamshid, prompt line (unchanged):** Jamshid, Tahmuras's son, at miniature scale. The house face, younger than Tahmuras: dark almond eyes, a long straight nose, a small closed mouth, a high smooth forehead. A short neat dark beard along the jaw only, not a long beard. Dark hair in two locks to the shoulders, held by a thin gold fillet. A narrow gold diadem, a simple band with one small plain gold disc at the front, never a tall crown and never a turban. A saffron brocade robe with small gold rosettes, a turquoise hem, a malachite sash, ivory trousers. No leopard skin. A soft pale-gold disc of farr behind the head, always dimmer than the painting's central light. He always faces the viewer's left.

**Jamshid, accession (only `01-taj`):** At his accession he is younger: the beard is only a faint dark line along the jaw, and the robe is plain saffron with a turquoise hem, not yet covered in rosettes.

**Jamshid, after the farr departs (only `17-far` and `18-tireh`):** The same face, the same robe, the same diadem. The farr disc is nearly gone: a thin grey-gold rim, dimmer than the central light, and the lapis around him is darker than in the glory paintings.

**Div host:** Wingless. Charcoal-black skin with a dull lapis sheen, not glossy. A wolf's muzzle, two white tusks, two short horns curving back. Eyes are two small vermilion dots. Three dark claws. No clothes. Only a little taller than a man. They face the viewer's right. They are labourers or throne-bearers here, not the dead div of Kayumars' story.

**Katuzian priests:** Small men in ivory robes with indigo hems and plain brown felt caps. No halos, no wings, no crowns. Not angels.

**Nisarian warriors:** Small men in short mail of tiny outlined rings, plain pointed iron helmets with no crest and no device, plain spears. No guns, no plate armour of later Europe.
