# 01.09 · آغاز کتاب → داستان دقیقی شاعر: illustration prompts

Twenty-five couplets. Readers have set their hearts on these stories. A young
poet, Daqiqi, promises to turn the book into verse, and the assembly rejoices.
His nature is troubled; death sets a dark helmet on him, and a slave's hand
kills him. The book stays unsaid. Ferdowsi turns from that empty desk toward
the throne of the king, then falters: time is turning, the treasure will not
keep, the age is full of war. A friend in his city brings the Pahlavi book,
and the poet's dark soul catches fire.

The journey: a waiting lamp, the book being read, Daqiqi's speech-flame, a
dying flame under a helmet, a prayer lamp in an empty arch, a distant empty
throne, the wheel of days, a lamp in a narrow pass, the friend's wrapped
book, and the book breaking into light.

The divine is never depicted. Couplet 10 is a prayer and couplet 19 mentions
a prophet: show prayer and guidance only as lamp-light and an empty path. No
God, no Prophet, no Ali, no angel, no halo, no resurrection bodies.

Recurring figures, repeated in full in every prompt:

- Daqiqi: a youth of about twenty, smooth chin, a thin new mustache, eager
  eyes, a saffron-yellow short tunic, vermilion trousers, a dark green cap
  with one small gold bead, no turban. Lively when alive. Distinct from the
  older poet.
- The poet (Ferdowsi): a small man of about forty, slender, olive skin, a
  long narrow black beard with two silver streaks at the chin, calm eyes, a
  plain white turban, a deep indigo robe with a thin gold hem and a turquoise
  cuff, ivory slippers. No crown.
- The friend: a kindly man of about fifty, a rounded face, a grey-streaked
  short beard, a turquoise coat, a cream turban.

God, no Prophet, no Ali, no angels, no halos. Books and papers show only
blank pages or nonlinguistic geometric ornaments, never writing, letters or

### 00-majles: threshold

SUBJECT: A night majlis prepared and still. A ring of flat cushions on a
carpet, a closed book whose cover is a gold geometric medallion with no
letters, and a single gold lamp. A lapis tiled pavilion, plane trees, a small
pool. No speaker. At most two tiny dim attendants at the far edges, not near
the light. Gold chi clouds, gold rosette stars.
COMPOSITION: The lamp flame is the brightest point at the exact centre.
Symmetrical. The closed book sits just below the lamp and is not brighter
than the flame.

### 01-khwanande: چو از دفتر این داستانها بسی / جهان دل نهاده بدین داستان

SUBJECT: A small reader in an ivory robe and white turban sits with an open
book. The pages show only geometric rosettes, never letters. A gold lamp
lights the book. Around him, in a shallow ring, small listeners lean in: an
old man, a youth, a woman seen from the side. A night garden court, tiled
floor, plane trees, lapis sky. Everyone is miniature and small.
COMPOSITION: The open book's glow and the lamp just above it form one bright
point at the exact centre. Listeners stay in the lower half and are dimmer
than the page.

### 02-daqiqi: جوانی بیامد گشاده زبان / به شعر آرم این نامه را گفت من

SUBJECT: Daqiqi, a youth of about twenty, smooth chin, thin new mustache,
saffron-yellow short tunic, vermilion trousers, a dark green cap with one
gold bead, no turban, stands small in the same night majlis. One hand is
raised. Just above the hand floats a white-gold almond of light, his speech,
with no face inside it. Small listeners around him brighten, hands lifted. An
open book of geometric ornament lies on the carpet. The older poet in indigo
is not in this painting.
COMPOSITION: The speech-flame is the brightest point at the exact centre,
just above Daqiqi's hand. He is small and below it. Listeners stay at the
sides, dimmer than the flame.

### 03-targ: برو تاختن کرد ناگاه مرگ / به دست یکی بنده بر کشته شد

SUBJECT: The same youth, Daqiqi, saffron tunic and dark green cap, has fallen
small on a carpet, eyes closed, peaceful, not wounded to look at. No blood
and no gore. Above him hovers a dark iron helmet, lamp-black with a dull rim.
The helmet is an object, not a person, not an angel, and it has no face.
At the side a single servant's hand and a short dagger, small, the blow
already done. A lamp has tipped; its last thin gold flame is almost out. The
majlis is empty. Night.
COMPOSITION: The last thin flame of the tipped lamp is the brightest point
at the exact centre. The dark helmet hangs directly above it and is darker,
not a second light. The fallen youth is below centre, small. The hand stays
inside the picture but away from the flame.

### 04-nagofte: برفت او و این نامه ناگفته ماند / الهی عفو کن گناه ورا

SUBJECT: An abandoned writing desk at night. A closed book, its cover dark,
no glow and no letters. A reed pen set down. In front of an empty pointed
arch, a single oil lamp burns with a quiet steady flame. The arch contains
nobody: no god, no angel, no face, no halo, no body. The flame is a prayer
addressed to an absence. Low in the lapis sky one small gold star is dim, as
if asleep. Stillness, paper grain, gold craquelure.
COMPOSITION: The prayer lamp's flame is the brightest point at the exact
centre, inside the empty arch. The dark book lies below, unlit. No person.
Strong symmetry.

### 05-takht: دل روشن من چو برگشت ازوی / که این نامه را دست پیش آورم

SUBJECT: The poet, a small man of about forty, long narrow black beard with
two silver streaks at the chin, plain white turban, deep indigo robe with a
thin gold hem and a turquoise cuff, ivory slippers, no crown, stands in a
courtyard at night. He has turned away from a dark writing desk behind him.
He holds a closed geometric book to his chest and a reed pen. Across the
court, through a distant iwan, an empty throne glows white-gold. No king is
seated. No divine figure. The throne's light is the hope he turns toward.
COMPOSITION: The empty throne's glow is the brightest point at the exact
centre, small and far inside the iwan. The poet is in the lower third, on
the centre line, walking toward it. The desk behind him is dim.

### 06-zaman: بپرسیدم از هر کسی بیشمار / سخن را نهفته همی داشتم

SUBJECT: The same poet, indigo robe, white turban, silver-streaked narrow
beard, stands small at a night crossroads. He holds a book wrapped in dark
cloth against his chest, hidden. Above him a great gold armillary of turning
rings, the days, with a small white-gold light at the hub. A few dim figures
at the sides turn away. An open chest nearby is empty. A cypress bends a
little in the wind. Lapis night, spongy rocks. No letters on the wrapped book.
COMPOSITION: The hub of the turning rings is the brightest point at the exact
centre. The poet is small, lower centre, and the wrapped book is dark. The
rings reach toward the edges. The empty chest is dim.

### 07-tang: سراسر زمانه پر از جنگ بود / اگر نامدی این سخن از خدای

SUBJECT: A narrow mountain pass at night between stacked spongy rocks. On
both ridges, kept dim and small, the signs of an age of war: banners, spears
and a few miniature elephants as silhouettes. No battle gore, no blood. In
the pass the same poet, indigo robe and white turban, very small, holds up a
single oil lamp. The lamp throws a short empty path of gold on the ground
ahead of him. Nobody stands on that path. No god, no prophet, no angel. The
lamp is speech and guidance shown only as light.
COMPOSITION: The lamp flame is the brightest point at the exact centre. The
poet is just below it, small. The armies stay outside the brightest area and
are darker than the flame. The lit path leads from the flame downward and is
not a second sun.

### 08-dust: به شهرم یکی مهربان دوست بود / نبشته من این نامه‌ی پهلوی

SUBJECT: A night street of brick and tile in a Persian city, one ordinary
lantern. Two small men stand so close their shoulders touch. One is the poet:
about forty, long narrow black beard with two silver streaks, white turban,
deep indigo robe. The other is the friend: about fifty, rounded kindly face,
grey-streaked short beard, turquoise coat, cream turban. The friend holds out
a book wrapped in cream cloth and tied with a saffron cord. The cloth glows
faintly from inside. No letters. A wooden door behind them.
COMPOSITION: The wrapped book's faint gold glow is the brightest point at the
exact centre, between their hands. Both figures are small, lower centre. The
street lantern is dimmer than the book.

### 09-afrukht: چو آورد این نامه نزدیک من / برافروخت این جان تاریک من

SUBJECT: A dark interior room at night. The friend, turquoise coat and cream
turban, has just set an opened book on a low stand before the poet. The poet
is the same man of about forty, indigo robe, white turban, silver-streaked
narrow beard. The pages show only geometric ornament, never letters, and from
them rises a tall white-gold flame of light, illumination rather than a
destructive fire. Gold sparks rise. The poet's face and robe catch the light.
The corners of the room stay dark lapis.
COMPOSITION: The flame rising from the open book is the brightest point at
the exact centre. The poet is small, just below, looking into it. The friend
steps back, smaller, to one side.
