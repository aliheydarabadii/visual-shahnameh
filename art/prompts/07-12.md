# 07.12 · فریدون → زادن منوچهر از مادر

Forty couplets. Time passes. In Iraj's rooms Fereydun finds Mah-Afarid, the handmaid Iraj loved, and she is with child. A daughter is born who looks like Iraj. When she is grown Fereydun betroths her to Pashang, and she bears a son. The boy is carried to his grandfather. The mobad says: look at Iraj. Fereydun laughs, sets the child on his lap, and names him Manuchehr from the bright face in the cup. The boy is reared so the wind may not touch him, taught a king's arts, and given the throne, the mace and the turquoise diadem. The treasury is opened. Champions come with vengeful hearts and praise him. On that feast the sheep is told from the wolf. Light moves from a harem lamp, to Mah-Afarid, to a cradle, to a betrothal cup, to the naming cup, to a parasol, to the new diadem, to a treasury key, to the young king above the flock.

Each centre is the doorway into the next painting. The brightest point is small, white-gold, and at the exact centre. People stay miniature. Everything that matters stays inside the central 45% of the width. Full-bleed: no border, frame, margin or signature. No letters in any script. Where the poem names God, the sky is only lapis and light: no face, no hand, no writing. No depiction of God, the Prophet or the Imams.

## Characters

**Fereydun** (sheet). A young king at miniature scale, of Abtin's house. Oval face, high forehead, dark almond eyes, a long straight nose, a small firm mouth, a faint vertical line between the brows. A short neat black beard along the jaw, not pointed and not white. Dark hair in two long locks past the shoulders. A low gold crown-cap, never a tall crown and never a turban: a smooth gold skullcap with one small plain gold disc at the front and one short black heron plume. An ivory kaftan patterned with a thin malachite vine, a narrow vermilion sash, ivory trousers, bare ankles. No leopard skin. A soft pale-gold disc of farr behind the head, always dimmer than the painting's central light. He always faces the viewer's left.

**Mah-Afarid** (sheet). A young handmaid at miniature scale, pari-faced only as a compliment: no wings, no halo. A rounder face than Iraj's daughter, dark almond eyes, a small closed mouth, black hair under a saffron veil worn back so the face is clear. A loose malachite-green robe with tiny gold flowers, modest, fully clothed, never nude. One small pearl at the ear. No crown, no farr. She always faces the viewer's left.

**Iraj's daughter** (sheet). A young woman at miniature scale who looks like Iraj. Oval face, dark almond eyes, a long straight nose, a small closed mouth, cheeks a pale arghavan rose. Black hair like musk in two long locks, a pale ivory veil edged in turquoise worn back so the face is clear. A rose-lavender robe with a thin gold hem, modest and fully clothed, never nude. A narrow gold fillet with no disc. No crown, no farr, no wings. She always faces the viewer's left.

**Manuchehr** (sheet). A youth of Iraj's house at miniature scale. Oval face, dark almond eyes, a long straight nose, a small closed mouth, cheeks a pale arghavan rose, no beard. Dark hair in two locks to the shoulders, held by a thin gold fillet with one small plain gold disc. An ivory tunic under a pale wine-coloured short coat with a turquoise hem. When enthroned, a narrow turquoise diadem: a simple band with one oval turquoise and one plain gold disc, never a tall crown. A heavy gold mace with a small flat ox-head, never larger than his torso, set beside him only when the scene arms him. A soft pale-gold disc of farr behind the head, always dimmer than the painting's central light. He always faces the viewer's left.
