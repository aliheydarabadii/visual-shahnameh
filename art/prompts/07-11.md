# 07.11 · فریدون → آوردن تابوت ایرج به نزد فریدون

Fifty couplets. Fereydun has prepared a welcome he will not give: a turquoise throne, jewels, wine, drums and elephants. Dark dust on the road brings a mourner and a gold coffin. The silk is drawn. The king falls from his horse, banners tear, drums turn over, and the army goes on foot to Iraj's garden. There the feast-place is empty. Fereydun burns the cypresses and prays, with no face in the sky, that someone of Iraj's seed will come for this vengeance. He weeps until a green shoot rests in his arms. The country sits in blue and black. Light moves from a jewel in a crown, into the dust, onto the coffin, onto a fallen diadem, into a bowl of water, onto an empty throne's reflection, into a small fire, into the shoot, through a shut door, and among the mourners.

Each centre is the doorway into the next painting. The brightest point is small, white-gold, and at the exact centre. People stay miniature. Everything that matters stays inside the central 45% of the width. Full-bleed: no border, frame, margin or signature. No letters in any script. Where the poem names God, the sky is only lapis and light: no face, no hand, no writing. No depiction of God, the Prophet or the Imams.

## Characters

**Fereydun** (sheet). A young king at miniature scale, of Abtin's house. Oval face, high forehead, dark almond eyes, a long straight nose, a small firm mouth, a faint vertical line between the brows. A short neat black beard along the jaw, not pointed and not white. Dark hair in two long locks past the shoulders. A low gold crown-cap, never a tall crown and never a turban: a smooth gold skullcap with one small plain gold disc at the front and one short black heron plume. An ivory kaftan patterned with a thin malachite vine, a narrow vermilion sash, ivory trousers, bare ankles. No leopard skin. A soft pale-gold disc of farr behind the head, always dimmer than the painting's central light. He always faces the viewer's left.

**Iraj** (sheet). The youngest son of Fereydun, a youth at miniature scale, slightly shorter than his brothers, the fairest of the three. The same long narrow face, high forehead, dark almond eyes, long straight nose and small closed mouth, with a smoother unbearded jaw. Dark hair in two long locks past the shoulders under a thin gold fillet with one small turquoise bead. An ivory robe with a pale-gold hem and a turquoise sash. No crown and no sword. Empty hands. A soft pale-gold disc of farr behind the head, always dimmer than the painting's central light. He always faces the viewer's left.
