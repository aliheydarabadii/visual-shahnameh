# 01.11 · آغاز کتاب → گفتار اندر ستایش سلطان محمود: illustration prompts

Forty-eight couplets in praise of Sultan Mahmud. No margrave like him has
appeared. The sun shows a crown and the earth turns to ivory. He sets his
throne upon the sun. The poet's sleeping star wakes; he lies a night with
praise on his lips and dreams a candle rising from the water, a lapis world
turning to yellow ruby, a turquoise throne. On it sits a king like the moon,
his army drawn up, seven hundred elephants, a vizier before him. The poet
asks whether this is the heavens or a court, and is told: the king of Rum and
Hind. Justice brings the sheep and the wolf to one water. He wakes, praises,
and having no coin scatters his soul; the world becomes a spring garden and
then rain. In feast he is eloquence, in war a dragon; his body an elephant,
his soul a light, his hand a rain-cloud, his heart a river. The loyal bind
their belts. His younger brother lives in his shade, and the commander of Tus
masters the lion and gives his coins away. The crown should endure. The poet
turns back to the book of kings.

The journey's light travels: a point on the horizon, the crowned sun, the
throne upon that sun, the poet's waking star, the candle from the water, the
crown among the elephants, the question, the shared water, dawn in a window,
spring blossom, a shaft of rain, the gold belts, the shade over the brother,
and at last the open book.

Sultan Mahmud, the same man in every painting he enters: a small miniature
king with a round moon-like face, a short neat black beard, a tall gold crown
topped by a sun-disc and a white plume, a white robe under a gold brocade
mantle, seated on a turquoise throne. He is not the young amir of the previous
section: no low diadem, no malachite-leaf robe.

The poet, whenever present: a small man of about forty, slender, olive skin,
a long narrow black beard with two silver streaks at the chin, a plain white
turban, a deep indigo robe with a thin gold hem and a turquoise cuff, ivory
slippers, no crown.

Never depict God, the Prophet, Ali, Gabriel, or any angel. Couplets that name
the creator, Yazdan, or Gabriel are painted as light, rain, a river, an
elephant, or an empty sky. No halo on any person. Farr is rays from the
crown, not a saint's disc.

### 00-ofoq: threshold

SUBJECT: A wide lapis night landscape with a high horizon and a pale ivory
earth still asleep. On the horizon, at the exact centre, a single point of
white-gold, the sun not yet risen. Gold rosette stars above. A few gold chi
cloud bands low. No person, no throne, no building large enough to compete.
COMPOSITION: The sun-point is the brightest element at the exact centre.
Vast calm sky. Symmetry. Full-bleed, still, before the praise begins.

### 01-khorshid: جهان آفرین تا جهان آفرید / چو خورشید بر چرخ بنمود تاج

SUBJECT: The wheel of heaven as a great gold ring on deep lapis. On the ring
a sun-disc wearing a small royal crown of gold with a white plume. The sun is
a disc, not a human face and not a god. Below, the earth is a pale ivory
plain catching the light, with a few tiny cypresses. No person anywhere. No
calligraphy. Gold rosette stars in the outer sky.
COMPOSITION: The crowned sun-disc is the brightest point at the exact centre.
The ring surrounds it. The ivory earth is the lower third. Symmetrical.

### 02-takht: ابوالقاسم آن شاه پیروزبخت / زخاور بیاراست تا باختر

SUBJECT: Sultan Mahmud, a small miniature king with a round moon-like face,
a short neat black beard, a tall gold crown topped by a sun-disc and a white
plume, a white robe under a gold brocade mantle, sits on a turquoise throne.
The throne rests on a great sun-disc, as if set upon the sun's crown. From
the lower edge of the disc a thin vein of gold ore opens in dark rock. Far
left and far right, two dim horizons of tiny cities, east and west. Lapis
night around the disc. No text, no angel.
COMPOSITION: The sun-disc and the throne's glow make one brightest point at
the exact centre. The king is small, seated inside that light. The gold vein
is lower and dimmer. The cities stay at the sides, tiny.

### 03-akhtar: مرا اختر خفته بیدار گشت / دل من چو نور اندر آن تیره شب

SUBJECT: A dark room. The poet, a small man of about forty, long narrow black
beard with two silver streaks, plain white turban, deep indigo robe, reclines
on a carpet, eyes closed, lips closed. Above him a single star that has just
woken burns white-gold. On his chest a small almond of inner light, the heart
like a lamp, not an angel and not a face. A closed geometric book near his
hand. No other people. Lapis night through a narrow window.
COMPOSITION: The waking star is the brightest point at the exact centre, just
above the poet. The heart-light is smaller and dimmer. He is small. The room
stays dark.

### 04-sham: چنان دید روشن روانم به خواب / یکی تخت پیروزه پیدا شدی

SUBJECT: Inside a dream, with no sleeper shown. Dark water fills the lower
half. From the water rises one tall white-gold candle flame, not a person.
Around it the lapis ground turns to yellow ruby and to flat brocade flowers,
a textile landscape, no letters. Behind the flame a turquoise throne is just
appearing, empty, with nobody on it. Gold rosette stars. No angel, no god.
COMPOSITION: The candle flame is the brightest point at the exact centre,
rising from the water. The empty throne sits directly behind it, slightly
dimmer, so the flame is the doorway to the throne. Symmetry.

### 05-pil: نشسته برو شهریاری چو ماه / یکی پاک دستور پیشش به پای

SUBJECT: The dream court at night. Sultan Mahmud, the same moon-like face,
short black beard, tall sun-crown with a white plume, white robe and gold
mantle, sits small on the turquoise throne. Before him stands a vizier: an
older man in a plain ivory robe and white turban, a slim staff, no halo, no
wings, no book. To one side a row of small Persian-miniature elephants, flat
and decorative, grey with vermilion and gold trappings, standing for a great
number. Tiny soldiers in two lines, very small. Lapis sky, gold chi clouds.
No text on banners.
COMPOSITION: The crown is the brightest point at the exact centre. Elephants
and soldiers stay lower and to the sides, dimmer than the crown. The vizier
is small, just below the throne.

### 06-porsesh: مرا خیره گشتی سر از فر شاه / یکی گفت کاین شاه روم است و هند

SUBJECT: The poet, tiny, indigo robe and white turban, stands in the lower
foreground with one hand raised in a question. Above him the same Sultan
Mahmud on the turquoise throne, radiant and small. A few nobles in a row, one
leaning as if answering. Behind the throne, rays of shell gold from the
crown, not a round halo. A thin horizon of tiny cities and a pale sea, Rum's
side and Hind's side, with no labels and no map text. A few miniature
elephants at the edge, dim. Lapis night.
COMPOSITION: The crown's rays are the brightest point at the exact centre.
The poet is small at the bottom centre. Nobles are dimmer. The horizon is a
thin band, not a second light.

### 07-mish: جهاندار محمود شاه بزرگ / به آبشخور آرد همی میش و گرگ

SUBJECT: A still night watering place. A round pool of pale gold water. At
its edge a white sheep and a grey wolf drink side by side, calm, neither
attacking, both small and miniature. Far behind, Sultan Mahmud on his
turquoise throne is tiny, his sun-crown a lesser light. One side of the
horizon is a mountain, the other a sea, with a few specks of distant kings
and no banners that can be read. Cypresses, lapis sky, gold rosettes. Peace.
COMPOSITION: The pool's bright reflection is the brightest point at the exact
centre. The sheep and the wolf are small, just below centre. The throne is
far and dimmer. No other light competes with the water.

### 08-bidari: چو کودک لب از شیر مادر بشست / چو بیدار گشتم بجستم ز جای

SUBJECT: A room at the edge of dawn. The poet is awake and standing, indigo
robe, white turban, silver-streaked beard, both hands lifted in praise toward
a window. Behind him a rumpled bed. At the side, very small, a wooden cradle
with a tiny child and a seated woman turned away, her face not a portrait.
The window holds a pale gold edge where the lapis sky is opening. No writing
anywhere, no name on the wall.
COMPOSITION: The pale gold of dawn in the window is the brightest point at
the exact centre. The poet stands below it on the centre line, small. The
cradle stays to the side.

### 09-bahar: ز فرش جهان شد چو باغ بهار / نبودم درم جان برافشاندم

SUBJECT: A spring garden at night, flowering: a plane tree in blossom, roses,
a stream, the ground patterned like a painted meadow. The poet, small, indigo
robe and white turban, stands with both hands open. From his hands drift
petals and gold dust, not coins and not a human figure of a soul. Soft
gold-edged clouds above. On the horizon a tiny crown-light, dimmer than the
blossoms. No text.
COMPOSITION: A cluster of white-gold blossoms at the exact centre is the
brightest point, where the dust from his hands arrives. The poet is lower
centre, small. The clouds are soft and not brighter than the flowers.

### 10-baran: از ابر اندرآمد به هنگام نم / به تن ژنده پیل و به جان جبرئیل

SUBJECT: Fine gold threads of rain fall from one cloud into a garden pool.
Around the pool: flowers, cypresses, and a stream that widens. Secondary and
small, at the sides only: a feast cup on a tray, a dragon-headed banner on a
pole (a standard, not a living monster), and one miniature elephant. There is
no angel, no wings, no halo and no human figure in the sky. A small inner
glow in the pool is only light. Sultan Mahmud is not in this frame.
COMPOSITION: The shaft of rain entering the pool is the brightest point at
the exact centre. The elephant, the cup and the banner stay at the sides and
are dimmer. No face in the light.

### 11-kamar: سر بخت بدخواه با خشم اوی / به فرمان ببسته کمر استوار

SUBJECT: A night court. A row of small standing men, some in noble robes and
some in plain servant dress, each with a gold belt buckled tight, heads
inclined. Sultan Mahmud, the same moon-like face and tall sun-crown, sits
small on the turquoise throne. At the far side one darkened figure holds a
single dull thin coin, his belt loose, turned away, not grotesque. Closed
chests of treasure stay dim: he is not looking at them. No text.
COMPOSITION: One central belt-buckle and the crown above it read as a single
brightest point at the exact centre. The dull coin is at the edge and dark.
The row is orderly and symmetrical.

### 12-baradar: نخستین برادرش کهتر به سال / و دیگر دلاور سپهدار طوس

SUBJECT: Sultan Mahmud, same face, tall sun-crown and turquoise throne, sits
small under a canopy. In the canopy's shade sits a younger prince: a shorter
beard, a simple gold cap and not a sun-crown, a green robe, peaceful. Beside
him stands the commander of Tus in a soldier's coat, a mace set down on the
ground, and a lion lying calm at his feet, miniature, not attacking. The
commander pours coins from his hand into a wide bowl. Seven small gold stars,
the Pleiades, sit above the throne and are dimmer than the centre. No names
and no writing.
COMPOSITION: A soft white-gold pool of light under the canopy is the brightest
point at the exact centre and falls on the younger brother. The sultan's
crown is just above it and is not a second sun. The lion and the coins are
lower and secondary.

### 13-bazgasht: کنون بازگردم به آغاز کار / سوی نامه‌ی نامور شهریار

SUBJECT: A quiet tiled room between a court and a book. In the background,
small, Sultan Mahmud's sun-crown and empty turquoise throne remain lit and
still, the wish that they endure, with the king himself tiny and calm if he
is shown at all. In the foreground the poet has turned: indigo robe, white
turban, silver-streaked beard. He opens the book of kings on a stand. The
pages are geometric ornament only, never letters, and a clear white-gold
light rises from the open page. A road of that light leads forward. No deity
and no angel.
COMPOSITION: The light of the open book is the brightest point at the exact
centre. The crown behind is smaller and dimmer. The poet is small, lower
centre, turned toward the book. This centre is a doorway back into the story.
