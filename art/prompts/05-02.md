# 05.02 · داستان مرداس → illustration prompts

One hundred and eighteen couplets. Jamshid's farr has just gone dark. The story
cuts to the Arab plain, where the good king Mardas keeps herds and gives milk
to anyone who needs it. His son Zahhak, called Bivarasp, swears an oath to
Iblis, and Iblis pits the garden path. Mardas falls at dawn and dies. Zahhak
takes the crown. Iblis returns as a cook, feeds him yolk, game, lamb and veal,
and asks to kiss his shoulders. Two black serpents grow there. As a physician,
Iblis orders human brains for them. Iran rises against Jamshid and hails the
serpent king. Jamshid flees for a hundred years, is found by the sea of China,
and is sawn in two. The poet counts seven hundred years and tires of the world.

The threshold follows 05.01's darkened court by leaving it: a walled garden
on the horsemen's plain, one lamp, no king yet. Each centre is the doorway
into the next painting. The brightest point is small, white-gold, and at the
exact centre. People stay miniature. Everything that matters stays inside the
central 45% of the width. Full-bleed: no border, frame, margin or signature.
No letters in any script. No depiction of God, the Prophet or the Imams.
When anyone prays, the sky is only lapis and gold light.

## Figures

**Mardas, Zahhak, Iblis, Jamshid.** Paste `art/characters/<name>.md`.

**Iblis disguises** (scene sentences, not a new prompt line). Counselor:
middle-aged, indigo coat, plain brown felt cap, a thin staff. Cook: appears
young, saffron tunic, white apron with only a geometric diamond, a plain
ladle. Physician: white robe, a small chest of jars with plain coloured
stoppers and no labels.

**Jamshid after the farr** (EXTRA, from his sheet): The same face, the same
robe, the same diadem. The farr disc is nearly gone: a thin grey-gold rim,
dimmer than the central light, and the lapis around him is darker than in
the glory paintings.

**Zahhak's state.** Through `15-buseh` he is beardless and has no serpents.
From `09-afsar` he wears the short black beard. From `17-mar` the two
serpents are present. He never wears Jamshid's diadem.

### 00-bagh: threshold

No characters.

SUBJECT: A walled garden on the night plain of Arab horsemen, before the
story enters. Spongy rocks in rose, lapis and ochre, one cypress, a strip of
pale sand running to the edges. A low mud-brick garden wall with a pointed
arch, no inscription. Inside the arch a path of pale earth and a few plane
leaves. A small gold lamp hangs in the arch, unattended. No people, no
animals, no pit yet.
COMPOSITION: The lamp's white-gold flame is at the exact centre and is the
only bright thing. The arch stays inside the central 45% width. Sand and
rocks run off every edge. Full-bleed, no border.
