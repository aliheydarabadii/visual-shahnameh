Persian miniature painting, Timurid–Safavid manuscript style (Herat/Tabriz
school, 15th–16th century), opaque mineral pigments and shell gold on
burnished paper, fine brushwork, flat stacked perspective, no Western
shading. Night palette: deep lapis-lazuli and ultramarine, glowing gold leaf,
small vermilion accents, malachite and turquoise, ivory. Visible paper grain
and slight gold craquelure.

SUBJECT: Night inside an open silk tent in the East. Salm and Tur sit small, both facing the viewer's left, each in a plain rounded gold cap. Between them a small oil lamp whose white-gold flame is the news of a distant kingship. A closed cylindrical case patterned with a geometric star lies at their feet, unopened. Through the tent flap a dark road and one far elephant, dim. Gold chi clouds along the lower silk. No writing anywhere.
COMPOSITION: The lamp's white core is at the exact centre of the frame and is the only bright thing, brighter than both gold caps and brighter than any farr. Both kings stay inside the central 45% width. Tent silk runs off every edge. Full-bleed, landscape 3:2. This lamp is the doorway into the road west.

**Salm.** The eldest son of Fereydun, a youth at miniature scale, the tallest of the three brothers. Long narrow face, high forehead, dark almond eyes, a long straight nose, a small closed mouth. A short neat dark beard. Dark hair to the shoulders under a thin gold fillet with one small malachite disc. A malachite-green robe over ivory, a gold belt, ivory trousers. No crown. A sheathed straight sword with a plain gold hilt. A soft pale-gold disc of farr behind the head, always dimmer than the painting's central light. He always faces the viewer's left. He rules Rum in this painting: add only a plain rounded gold cap over the fillet. No European crown.

**Tur.** The middle son of Fereydun, a youth at miniature scale, heavier in the shoulder than his brothers and still small in the frame. The same long narrow face, high forehead, dark almond eyes, long straight nose and small closed mouth. A short square dark beard. Dark hair to the jaw under a thin gold fillet with one small vermilion bead. A saffron robe, a vermilion sash, a gold belt, ivory trousers. No crown. A sheathed straight sword with a dark hilt. A soft pale-gold disc of farr behind the head, always dimmer than the painting's central light. He always faces the viewer's left. He rules the East in this painting: add only a plain rounded gold cap over the fillet. No European crown.

Strictly no text, letters, calligraphy, numerals, border, frame, margin, watermark or signature.
No depiction of God, the Prophet or the Imams. No face, no hand and no writing in the sky.
Figures are small, flat and miniature, never photoreal, never larger than a fifth of the frame height.
Not a 3D render, not a photograph, not fantasy concept art.
The single brightest point is a small white-gold core at the exact centre of the frame, brighter than every farr, jewel, moon and sun-disc.
