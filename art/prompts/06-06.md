# 06.06 · ضحاک → آهنگ جنگ فریدون با ضحاک: illustration prompts

Two hundred and two couplets. The section before this one ends with Kaveh's smiths
putting the buffalo-headed mace in Fereydun's hand. This one is the march.
Fereydun lifts his head to the sun on Khordad day, the army gathers, and the
Arvand ferrymen refuse him. He rides Golrang into the river. At Gang-dezh,
Zahhak's palace, he breaks the talisman, sits the throne, and hears Arnavaz
and Shahrnaz. Kondrow feasts him, then tells Zahhak. Zahhak comes by night
with a host of divs. The city rains stones. From the roof Zahhak drops with
a dagger; Fereydun's mace breaks the helmet; Sorush forbids the killing.
Fereydun binds him with a lion's hide, sends the city back to its crafts,
and nails him in a cave on Damavand. The world is washed of his name.

Thirty-four paintings plus the threshold. Each centre is the doorway into
the next. The brightest point is small, white-gold, and at the exact centre.
People stay miniature. Everything that matters stays inside the central 45%
of the width. Full-bleed: no border, frame, margin or signature. No letters
in any script, no numerals, no inscriptions on tile, cloth, metal, leather
or sky. Books and seals, if any, show only a geometric star.

The divine is never a figure. When Fereydun calls on the Creator, the sky is
only lapis and gold light: no face, no hand, no writing. No depiction of
God, the Prophet or the Imams.

## Style wrapper

Persian miniature painting, Timurid–Safavid manuscript style (Herat/Tabriz
school, 15th–16th century), opaque mineral pigments and shell gold on
burnished paper, fine brushwork, flat stacked perspective, no Western
shading. Night palette: deep lapis-lazuli and ultramarine, glowing gold leaf,
small vermilion accents, malachite and turquoise, ivory. Visible paper grain
and slight gold craquelure.

## Closing

Strictly no text, letters, calligraphy, numerals, border, frame, margin,
watermark or signature. Not a 3D render, not photographic, not fantasy
concept art. No depiction of God, the Prophet, or the Imams. No face, hand,
or writing in the sky. Ornament is geometric stars and eslimi scrolls only.
Output exactly 1536×1024, landscape 3:2. The white-gold core sits at the
exact centre of the frame.

## Character lines

Paste the named line unchanged, then the EXTRA sentence for that painting.

**Fereydun.** A young king at miniature scale, of Abtin's house. Oval face, high forehead, dark almond eyes, a long straight nose, a small firm mouth, a faint vertical line between the brows. A short neat black beard along the jaw, not pointed and not white. Dark hair in two long locks past the shoulders. A low gold crown-cap, never a tall crown and never a turban: a smooth gold skullcap with one small plain gold disc at the front and one short black heron plume. An ivory kaftan patterned with a thin malachite vine, a narrow vermilion sash, ivory trousers, bare ankles. No leopard skin. A soft pale-gold disc of farr behind the head, always dimmer than the painting's central light. He always faces the viewer's left.

**Zahhak.** A tall prince of the Arabs at miniature scale. Warm olive skin, a long narrow face, high cheekbones, heavy-lidded dark almond eyes, a long straight nose, a small closed mouth. Black hair to the shoulders, held by a narrow gold fillet with one oval garnet. Before he is crowned, no beard. After he is crowned, a short neat black beard along the jaw only, never a full beard and never white. A vermilion robe with a gold hem under a black cloak lined in saffron. One plain gold arm-ring on the right wrist. He always faces the viewer's right. Before the serpents, both shoulders are bare of snakes. After they grow, two small flat black serpents rise from the two shoulders, heads turned inward toward his neck, each eye a tiny vermilion dot, no wings and no scales painted as armour.

**Arnavaz.** Jamshid's younger daughter at miniature scale. Fair ivory skin, a rounder face than her sister, the same arched dark brows, the same long straight nose, dark almond eyes, a small mouth, and no beauty mark. Black hair loose under a shell-pink veil. An ivory robe with a malachite hem and one thin gold necklace with a single turquoise drop. No crown and no diadem. She always faces the viewer's left.

**Shahrnaz.** Jamshid's elder daughter at miniature scale. Fair ivory skin, an oval face, arched dark brows, dark almond eyes, a long straight nose, a small mouth. A tiny dark beauty mark on the visible cheek. Black hair in one long braid ending in a plain gold tube. A saffron veil over a turquoise robe with a narrow vermilion sash. No crown and no diadem. She always faces the viewer's left.

**Kiyanush.** Fereydun's elder brother, a young man at miniature scale. The same oval face, high forehead, dark almond eyes and long straight nose, with a slightly heavier jaw. A thin dark beard. Dark hair to the shoulders, a thin gold fillet with no disc and no plume. No crown and no farr. An ivory tunic with a brown hem, and a plain brown cloak. He always faces the viewer's left.

**Shadkam.** Fereydun's other elder brother, a young man at miniature scale, called the precious one. The same oval face as Kiyanush, with rounder cheeks and a thinner dark beard. Dark hair to the shoulders, a thin gold fillet with no disc and no plume. No crown and no farr. An ivory tunic with a turquoise hem, and no cloak. He always faces the viewer's left.

**Kondrow.** Kondrow, Zahhak's steward, at miniature scale. A short middle-aged man, round face, thin brown beard to the jaw, narrow eyes, a small mouth. No crown, no fillet, no plume, no farr. A rust-brown knee-length coat, an ivory skullcap with no disc, ivory trousers, and a plain cloth of office at the belt with one geometric star and no writing. A slight stoop. No weapon. He always faces the viewer's left.

**Golrang.** Golrang, Fereydun's horse, at miniature scale. A rose-madder coat, not neon and not chestnut-brown, an ivory mane and tail, dark hooves, a gold bridle with three turquoise beads and no inscription. Flat manuscript horse, not a photographed animal. He faces the viewer's left, the same way as his rider.

**Sorush.** Not an angel of religious art, and not a divine person. A slender pari: an oval calm face, dark eyes, no beard, no crown, no halo. A short leopard cloak, paler tawny than the king's, with the same black ringed rosettes. Two narrow gold wings drawn as arabesque leaves, not bird feathers. No weapon. Small.

**Barmayeh.** A cow at flat manuscript scale, not a living animal and not a heraldic bull. Slender. An ivory ground coat patterned all over with small peacock-eye feathers in turquoise, malachite, saffron and a very little vermilion. Two short plain gold horns curving forward, with no inscription. A small pale-gold star on the brow, always dimmer than the painting's central light. One dark almond eye, outlined, calm, not a human face. No wings, no halo, no cloth. Alive, she stands. Struck down, she lies with legs folded and head lowered, the pattern unchanged, and with no blood.

**Div host.** Not the one black div of Kayumars. Each is wingless, charcoal-black with a dull lapis sheen, a wolf muzzle, two white tusks, two short horns, vermilion-dot eyes, three claws, no clothes. Only a little taller than a man, flat manuscript scale, facing the viewer's right.

**Banner.** A plain spear with Kaveh's dark-brown leather apron tied on it, and a little saffron and turquoise cloth beside the leather, no writing. Dimmer than the central light.

**Mace.** A steel mace with a flat stylized buffalo head, no inscription.
