# 07.10 · فریدون → کشتن برادران، ایرج را

Forty-three couplets. At dawn Salm and Tur go to Iraj's tent. He runs to them in love. Tur charges him with taking the crown. Iraj yields Iran, the west and China, and says his only custom is to be the younger. Tur is not pleased. He seizes the gold chair and strikes. Iraj begs for his life and for their father's heart. Tur does not answer. A blue dagger ends the young king. With restraint the paintings show a fallen cypress, not a wound: the neck stays hidden. Musk and a wrapped casket go back to the old king, and the two brothers ride opposite ways. Light moves from a dawn slit, to Iraj running into it, to an unwanted crown, to open hands, to Tur's knitted brow, to the raised chair, to the plea, to the fallen fillet, to the musk casket.

Each centre is the doorway into the next painting. The brightest point is small, white-gold, and at the exact centre. People stay miniature. Everything that matters stays inside the central 45% of the width. Full-bleed: no border, frame, margin or signature. No letters in any script. Where the poem names God, the sky is only lapis and light: no face, no hand, no writing. No depiction of God, the Prophet or the Imams.

## Characters

**Iraj** (sheet). The youngest son of Fereydun, a youth at miniature scale, slightly shorter than his brothers, the fairest of the three. The same long narrow face, high forehead, dark almond eyes, long straight nose and small closed mouth, with a smoother unbearded jaw. Dark hair in two long locks past the shoulders under a thin gold fillet with one small turquoise bead. An ivory robe with a pale-gold hem and a turquoise sash. No crown and no sword. Empty hands. A soft pale-gold disc of farr behind the head, always dimmer than the painting's central light. He always faces the viewer's left.

**Salm** (sheet). The eldest son of Fereydun, a youth at miniature scale, the tallest of the three brothers. Long narrow face, high forehead, dark almond eyes, a long straight nose, a small closed mouth. A short neat dark beard. Dark hair to the shoulders under a thin gold fillet with one small malachite disc. A malachite-green robe over ivory, a gold belt, ivory trousers. No crown. A sheathed straight sword with a plain gold hilt. A soft pale-gold disc of farr behind the head, always dimmer than the painting's central light. He always faces the viewer's left.

**Tur** (sheet). The middle son of Fereydun, a youth at miniature scale, heavier in the shoulder than his brothers and still small in the frame. The same long narrow face, high forehead, dark almond eyes, long straight nose and small closed mouth. A short square dark beard. Dark hair to the jaw under a thin gold fillet with one small vermilion bead. A saffron robe, a vermilion sash, a gold belt, ivory trousers. No crown. A sheathed straight sword with a dark hilt. A soft pale-gold disc of farr behind the head, always dimmer than the painting's central light. He always faces the viewer's left.
